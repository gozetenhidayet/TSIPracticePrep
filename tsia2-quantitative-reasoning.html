TSI Math Practice Test — Quantitative Reasoning

Ratios, proportions, percentages, and other quantitative relationships used in everyday and academic contexts.

Practice TSIA2 Quantitative Reasoning →

What this skill covers

Quantitative Reasoning questions on the TSIA2 focus on using numbers sensibly in context — scaling a ratio up or down, computing a percentage change, or working with a proportional relationship described in words.

The most reliable approach for a ratio or proportion question is to find the scale factor between the given amount and the stated ratio, then apply that same factor to the other quantity.

Worked examples

Example 1
A recipe uses 2 cups of flour for every 5 cups of water. If 8 cups of flour are used, how many cups of water are needed?
A. 20
B. 16
C. 10
D. 12
Why: 8 cups of flour is 4 times the 2-cup ratio unit (8 ÷ 2 = 4), so water is also scaled by 4: 5 × 4 = 20 cups.
Example 2
An item priced at $60 is marked up by 15%. What is the new price?
A. $69
B. $75
C. $51
D. $45
Why: 15% of $60 is $9, so the new price is $60 + $9 = $69 — watch for the sign: a markup adds, a discount subtracts.
