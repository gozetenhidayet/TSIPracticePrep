ACT Conventions of Standard English

Grammar, usage, sentence structure, and punctuation rules — the largest single category on the ACT English test.

Practice ACT Conventions of Standard English →

What this skill covers

This is the ACT's largest English category, covering subject-verb agreement, pronoun agreement, verb tense consistency, and punctuation (commas, apostrophes, semicolons) used correctly.

A reliable check for agreement questions: cross out any prepositional phrase between the subject and the verb (like "of the students") — what's left is the true subject the verb must agree with.

Worked examples

Example 1
Which choice is correct? "Each of the students have submitted their assignment."
A. Each of the students has submitted the assignment.
B. Each of the students have submitted the assignment.
C. Each of the students having submitted the assignment.
D. Each of the students were submitting the assignment.
Why: "Each" is singular no matter how many students follow it, so "has" is required, not "have".
Example 2
Choose the correctly punctuated sentence.
A. The report, which took three weeks to finish, impressed the board.
B. The report which took three weeks to finish, impressed the board.
C. The report, which took three weeks to finish impressed the board.
D. The report which took three weeks to finish impressed, the board.
Why: A nonessential clause ("which took three weeks to finish") needs a comma on both sides, matching how it is set off in the sentence.
